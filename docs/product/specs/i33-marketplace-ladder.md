> **Kind:** a decision record, not a build plan. It records the owner's ambition, a six-stage path, the gates between stages and the evidence each gate needs. Nothing here is scheduled for building.
> **Related:** stages 1 to 3 are built in [I-32](i32-seller-handoff-and-affiliate-carts.md) on top of [I-06](../roadmap.md#i-06-collection-and-purchase-planning). The non-goal this record could reverse sits in the roadmap's [deliberate non-goals](../roadmap.md#deliberate-non-goals-from-the-benchmark).
> **Research:** dated notes of 2026-11-09 on marketplaces, launch strategy, EU and French law, costs and affiliates. They are not committed, so every external fact cites a numbered entry in **Sources**. Legal points are research, not legal advice.

## Overview

The owner's ambition, stated on 2026-11-09 and translated from French:

> "Software more beautiful and more complete than Moxfield, more information than EDHREC; be one of the deckbuilding references, maybe even a marketplace recognised like Cardmarket or TCGplayer."

The roadmap says otherwise today. Its deliberate non-goals read: "Do not build a draft simulator, cube platform, venue directory or full card-trading marketplace in the current roadmap." Its 2027 review adds: "I-06 hands off to sellers; it does not become one." This record does not change that non-goal. Reversing it is the owner's decision alone. The record holds three things:

- a six-stage ladder, from affiliate links to an open player-to-player marketplace, where each stage is useful alone and the product can stop on any rung;
- the stage that would reverse the non-goal: **stage 5**, the first stage where a buyer pays a seller through MagicAIBuilder;
- for each step up, the measurable evidence that must exist first, and the evidence that stops the climb.

The research's central finding: deckbuilders partner with marketplaces, and none of the major ones found runs its own [3][4][6]. Mana Pool, the one new marketplace with visible traction, grew by courting deckbuilders [1][3]. MagicAIBuilder already holds the demand side: I-06's acquisition plan says exactly which printings a player needs. The question is when, if ever, holding the money as well is worth what it costs.

## Current state in this codebase

Verified on `dev` on 2026-11-09, at commit `49983a9`:

- **No checkout, cart, payment or billing code.** `grep -rniE "stripe|checkout|payment|cart" src/` matches only the Comprehensive Rules text in `src/lib/rules/data/MagicCompRules-20260807.txt`, the word for "card" in other locales ("carte", "carta") and lucide's `ShoppingCart` icon on the shopping-list button. `prisma/schema.prisma` has no order, seller, payout or payment model. `package.json` lists no payment SDK.
- **No seller links.** No file under `src/` or `prisma/` names TCGplayer, Cardmarket, Mana Pool, Card Kingdom or CardTrader. Nothing reads Scryfall's `purchase_uris`, sets an affiliate parameter or marks a link `sponsored`.
- **Purchase planning ends at a list (I-06).** `src/lib/collection/acquisition-plan.ts` aggregates missing printings across decks without changing ownership; `src/app/api/collection/acquisition-plan/route.ts` serves it and `src/components/collection/AcquisitionPlanPanel.tsx` shows it. `src/lib/collection/acquisition-csv.ts` exports names, quantities, USD prices and Scryfall printing IDs; `generateShoppingListCSV` in `src/lib/deck/pricing.ts` exports one deck's list. The `AcquisitionIntent` model stores a private `proxy_now` or `buy_later` choice per printing.
- **Prices are USD only.** `src/lib/deck/pricing.ts` states that "every ingest path reads Scryfall's `prices.usd`". `src/lib/scryfall/types.ts` declares `eur`, and no code reads it.
- **No product analytics.** The only telemetry dependency is `@sentry/nextjs`, for errors. Nothing counts outbound clicks, so no gate below can be measured yet.

## Functional requirements

Requirements here are decision gates and the evidence each gate needs. None is a feature.

### 1. The decision and its boundaries

1.1 The roadmap non-goal "full card-trading marketplace" stays in force until the owner records a stage-5 "go" in the decision log (group 6). No spec, slice or agent implements stage 5 or 6 before that entry exists.
1.2 Stages 1 to 4 fit the current non-goal: MagicAIBuilder never holds buyer money, never presents a seller's offer as its own and never onboards sellers.
1.3 Stage 5 reverses the non-goal. The owner's "go" entry and a roadmap PR that rewrites the non-goal land together.
1.4 **Advice independence, at every stage.** No card suggestion, ranking, bracket estimate or copilot answer reads affiliate, commission or seller data. A budget limit the player sets is the only price input to advice. The roadmap's ban on "engagement loops that rank visibility by spend" covers sellers too: no paid placement.
1.5 Card data and fan content stay free at every stage. Scryfall forbids a paywall on its data, and every price shown today comes from Scryfall [15].

### 3. Legal duties by stage (research, not legal advice)

3.1 **Stages 1–4:** a disclosure on every affiliate link, as EDHREC's terms disclose theirs [6], and each price source's display terms. The Fan Content Policy names "sponsorships, ad revenue, and donations", not affiliate links [14]; whether it treats them as ad revenue is a lawyer question.
3.2 **Stage 5 adds:** notice and action under the DSA (Art. 16, not re-verified in this research). Its marketplace duties (Arts. 30–32: trader identity checks, compliance by design) do not apply to platforms that are micro or small enterprises under Recommendation 2003/361/EC, with 12 months' grace after outgrowing it [16]; the thresholds were not verified. Seller-status disclosure under Omnibus Art. 6a, from 2022-05-28 [17]; French L111-7 does not apply when every offeror is a professional [18]. A 14-day withdrawal right on purchases from professionals, extended up to 12 months if the buyer is not told [19]. DAC7: an annual seller report by 31 January, sellers under 30 sales and €2,000 excluded, and no platform-size exemption found [20]. GPSR Art. 22 duties since 2024-12-13 [21]; whether trading cards count as toys was not established. P2B rules on terms toward sellers [22]. Payment: the EBA and BaFin read marketplaces as outside PSD2's commercial-agent exclusion [23], and the PSD3/PSR compromise of 2026-04-24 tightens it further [24], so funds must sit with a licensed provider.
3.3 **Stage 6 adds:** telling buyers that EU consumer rights do not apply to a private seller [17]; no withdrawal-right source covers private sales [19]; more sellers cross the DAC7 thresholds [20]. Presenting items as the platform's own may cost the hosting liability shield (DSA Art. 6(3), not re-verified in this research).

**Computed** (assumptions, not sourced data: €1M of sales in 20,000 orders, 100 sellers paid weekly, standard EEA cards, Stripe Connect France [25]). Card processing is 1.5% plus €0.25 per order, about €20,000. Payouts are 0.25% plus €0.10 per payout, about €3,000. Active sellers cost €2 each a month, €2,400. If MagicAIBuilder absorbs card processing, €50,000 gross leaves about €24,600. If sellers pay it, as Mana Pool's sellers pay 2.9% + 30¢ [1], it leaves about €44,600. Either way it covers at most one support salary at Mana Pool's posted $45,000 [5], before French employer charges, which were not researched. For scale, Cardmarket's estimated US$566M [8] at 5% is about US$28M.

At equal sales, a 5% take and a 5% affiliate rate earn the same gross. After payment costs the marketplace keeps about half to nine-tenths of it, roughly one support salary at Mana Pool's posted level, while it carries the legal stack and must build supply. What it buys is control and income on repeat orders, which affiliate windows of 48 hours or 7 days do not pay [2][10]. Partnering leaves the product exposed to a partner's terms. Neither path alone makes MagicAIBuilder "recognised like Cardmarket", which was founded in 2007 and has 80+ staff [6][7].

**Paid tier (I-30).** Players pay elsewhere for price alerts: MTGGoldfish sells unlimited alerts and TopDecked a "Pricing Professional" tier [27]. Moxfield, Archidekt and EDHREC hand off to sellers at no charge to players [3][4][6]. Stage 4 alerts are therefore the only rung with a plausible paid feature, and only on a feed whose terms allow it, since Scryfall forbids paywalling its data [15]. Affiliate and take-rate income are alternatives to charging, never a charge on card data.

## Non-goals

- Building anything for stage 5 or 6 before the owner's "go" is logged.
- Changing the roadmap's non-goal in the PR that adds this record.
- An in-house wallet, escrow or store credit, at any stage. Funds held by MagicAIBuilder would need a payment or e-money licence.
- A warehouse or consolidation hub like TCGplayer Direct or CardTrader Zero, or selling cards from MagicAIBuilder's own stock. Either would be a separate decision after stage 6.
- Paid placement of sellers, decks or cards.
- Scraping seller prices, or using a price API against its terms.

## Testing strategy

- **Pre-registration:** definitions and thresholds are committed before measuring (6.2). A review that changes a threshold to pass a gate is invalid.
- **Reproducibility:** each gate figure comes from a dated export attached to the review. A second person recomputes the same number.
- **I-32 tests this record relies on:** one handoff is counted once; no click record keeps a player identity beyond the monthly count; every seller link renders its disclosure; copilot and suggestion output are identical with affiliate configuration on and off.
- **Legal acceptance:** a dated memo from a French lawyer answers each stage-5 question in the open questions.
- **Wind-down drill:** before any stage-5 launch, a written closure plan covering refunds, the DAC7 report and seller notices is reviewed.

## Open questions

- **Are the proposed thresholds right?** €1M of attributed yearly sales, 1,000 monthly buyers and 80% cart coverage are proposals. The owner sets them before slice 2 starts measuring.
- **Which market first?** France sits in Cardmarket's home market, where no fee gap was found. Mana Pool grew in the US against an incumbent charging over 10%, and whether it ships to France was not verified.
- **Lawyer questions for stage 5**, added to the twelve from I-29's research: is a solo-run pilot a micro or small enterprise under DSA Art. 29? Does a professional-only pilot escape L111-7? Which GPSR and toy-safety duties apply to single cards? Which P2B and VAT deemed-supplier rules apply? Do affiliate links fit the Fan Content Policy? Does a listing that shows card images fit it?
- **What does Mana Pool's "first sale" mean:** the first order after each click, or a referred customer's first order ever? The second makes repeat buyers worth nothing to an affiliate.
- **Would Cardmarket sign a data or affiliate partnership** with a small deckbuilder? EDHREC's is the only one documented [6].
